### Introduction

The RCPCH Incubator had very humble beginnings as simply two clinicians-who-code (@pacharanero and @eatyourpeas, hired by the RCPCH to build the original Digital Growth Charts platform.

Even then, it was important to us to try to write down some of the culture we wanted the Incubator to embody, some industry best practices, some key decisions we'd made --- such as our commitment to open source --- and other 'metaprogramming'-level considerations.

What we are doing here is different to 'just normal tech' - it cuts across software development, medicine, information governance, clinical software, medical culture, safety, security, politics, commercial considerations, and medical devices. It's only right that we might need to consciously consider how we do things at the Incubator, and for sometimes those things to be different from the rest of the tech world (and the rest of the NHS!)

We now have a small team behind the Incubator and numerous projects. We expect this document to be iteratively improved and to reflect our current and future development practices.


## Background





## Why Do We Need A Playbook?

There exist a multitude of technological solutions to solve problems in healthcare. Every option has pros and cons, and the field is constantly developing.

Rather than getting lost in the choice paradox, we try to narrow the field of options to things we know work. In a similar way to how medical Life Support courses (APLS, ATLS, ALS, etc) show you '**one** good way' (when many other ways exist) to manage a critically ill patient, we're going to show you The Incubator Way. Other ways might also get the job done, but we need to all know that the team can work together and do it The Incubator Way.

Though we derive our methods from the wider tech world's best practices, we have adapted them to fit the unique needs of healthcare. Similar to the differences between *health* economics and general economics, *health* technology is unique in a few distinct ways:

- Regulation and strict safety standards
- Need for transparency, accuracy, and reliability
- More significant impact and value delivery

## Regulation and Strict Safety Standards

Healthcare technology is specifically designed to support and improve healthcare services and patient outcomes. It includes a range of hardware, software and systems used to diagnose, treat, and monitor medical conditions.

> These tools impact lives.

For this reason, they must be subject to high levels of regulation whilst meeting strict safety and quality standards.

## Transparency, Openness, Accuracy and Reliability

The consequences of mistakes in healthcare technology can have serious consequences.

Like peer review in academia, our tools should be subject to scrutiny and independent verification of performance to mitigate the risk of harm. In addition, principles like open source and interoperability ensure these products and services are safe and effective in clinical practice. They should improve the delivery of healthcare, not add increased barriers and friction.

> A black box should not decide a patient's life.

## Value delivery and impact

Though standards are necessarily higher, implementing healthcare technology enables value delivery and large-scale impact on a global scale.

> The future of medicine exists - but it's unevenly distributed.

Creating a robust, safe, and transparent foundation allows the democratisation of healthcare, improving everyone's lives.
